How the score is calculated

The score rewards fiber, protein and vegetable share, then subtracts glycemic load and net carbohydrate density. It is a teaching aid, not a prediction of your personal glucose response.

96 − 1.7·GL − 0.45·netCarbs + min(8, 0.55·fiber) + min(6, 0.08·protein) + 8·vegShare

Clamped to 0–100 and rounded. See NUTRITION.md for worked examples.

      The method, in plain language

      Why this plate works

      01

      Half the plate is non-starchy veg

      Volume, water, fiber and micronutrients with very little available carbohydrate. Broccoli, greens, cauliflower, zucchini and peppers do the heavy lifting.

      02

      A palm of protein

      Salmon, eggs, chicken or tofu anchor satiety. Protein does not raise glucose appreciably on its own and tends to blunt the next craving.

      03

      Fats and seeds, measured

      Avocado, nuts, olive oil and chia add mouthfeel and slow digestion. They are calorie-dense, so the plate keeps portions honest.

      04

      Starch stays off the plate

      Rice, potatoes, bread, pasta and fries are the fastest route to a post-meal spike. PlateWise redirects each one to a whole-food swap instead of shaming the craving.

      Receipts, not vibes

      Grounded in published evidence

      Nutrition numbers are per-serving estimates derived from USDA FoodData Central. Glycemic index values are drawn from published international tables and are used with their real-world variability in mind. The plate method and carbohydrate-aware advice follow the American Diabetes Association's Standards of Care and nutrition consensus report. Peer-reviewed carbohydrate-restriction research is cited for context, not as a promise.

      How PlateWise works

      1. 1
        Pick a whole food.

        The pantry only contains non-starchy vegetables, quality protein, healthy fats and low-GI berries. Search by name, category or craving.

      2. 2
        Watch the plate, not a spreadsheet.

        Each serving lands in its 50/25/25 zone. The HUD updates net carbs, fiber, glycemic load and a safety score immediately.

      3. 3
        Preview the curve.

        The glucose chart compares your plate with a reference rice-and-potato meal. It is a teaching model, not a CGM reading.

      4. 4
        Swap the craving.

        Rice becomes cauliflower rice. Pasta becomes zucchini noodles. Tap a swap to put the alternative on the plate.

      5. 5
        Save what worked.

        Meals are stored only in this browser's local storage. Export the log as JSON any time.
